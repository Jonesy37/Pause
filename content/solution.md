**ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SAFETY OVERSIGHT ACT**

**Section 1. General Provisions**
(1) As used in this chapter:
(a) “Artificial intelligence system” means a machine-based system that makes predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing real or virtual environments. 
(b) “Large Artificial Intelligence System” means an artificial intelligence system
	(i) whose total development cost exceeds $10,000,000; 
(ii) which utilizes over 10,000,000,000 parameters;
(iii) which was trained using a quantity of computing power greater than 10^24 integer or floating-point operations; or
(iv) which could reasonably be expected to have similar performance on benchmarks commonly used to quantify the performance of state-of-the-art foundation models 
(c) “Generative artificial intelligence” means an artificial intelligence system that:
(i) is trained on data;
(ii) interacts with a person using text, audio, or visual communication; and
(iii) generates non-scripted outputs similar to outputs created by a human, with limited or no human oversight. 
(d) “Generated content” means any content that is:
(i) fully or partially generated by a generative artificial intelligence; and
(ii) meaningfully altered by a generative artificial intelligence 
(e) “Committee” refers to the Utah Committee for Artificial Intelligence Safety Oversight created in this section
(f) “Air-gapped” means physically isolated from the public internet and from any external network such that no data can be transmitted to or from the system expect through controlled, logged, and authenticated transfer procedures
(g) Data Center: A facility used primarily to house computer systems and associated components, including servers, storage systems, and networking equipment, that is used in part or in whole for the training, deployment, or operation of an artificial intelligence system.
(h) “High-Consumption Data Center” refers to a data center that:
	(i) has an electrical capacity of 10 megawatts; or
	(ii) consumes 50 million gallons or more annually for operational purposes


**Section 2. Safety Standards**
(1) All developers of large artificial intelligence systems must adhere to strict safety guidelines before development is permitted.
(2) These standards apply to current and future large artificial intelligence systems
(3) These standards are that the developers of artificial intelligence systems must ensure:
(a) the ability to interpret the drives and choices of a given artificial intelligence system, so as to ensure the compliance with the other safety standards, including the ability to
	(i) understand the steps an artificial intelligence system took to arrive at a 
given output; and
(ii) describe the functional role of said steps
(b) the complete prevention of any system to self-replicate, meaning a complete prohibition on the ability to
(i) create copies of itself that are capable of performing tasks at the same or similar levels as the original;
(ii) create copies of model weights, code, or other essential functions that would enable another entity to easily produce similar capabilities to the original; and
(iii) distribute said copies to computer systems other than the one the original system exists on 
(c) strict model alignment with lawful purposes within any use case, meaning models must: 
(i)  be flexible enough to adapt to different legal thresholds, or else function at the minimum level of alignment; 
(ii) ensure the models are not deceptive when determining alignment; and
(iii) ensure the models cannot be exploited by users to accomplish illicit purposes
(d) the capability for reliable identification of any generated visual content that:
	(i) persists over light alterations to generated content
	(ii) can be accessed by the general public
(e) the ability to completely deactivate any system within 12 hours, including
	(i) any data center or computer system the original model runs on
(ii) any distributed copies of the model to private systems the original distributors maintain access to
(4) Any artificial intelligence model that does not meet these standards shall be prohibited from further development until such a time in which it meets these standards

**Section 3. Utah Committee for Artificial Intelligence Safety Oversight**
(1) There is established the Utah Committee for Artificial Intelligence Safety Oversight,
to be administered by the Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy.
(2) The purpose of the committee is to:
(a) perform audits on all approved artificial intelligence systems; and(b) ensure the security and safety compliance of each of these systems.
(3) The committee consists of 9 members appointed as follows:
(a) 3 members appointed by the governor;
(b) 3 members appointed by the president of the Senate; and
(c) 3 members appointed by the speaker of the House of Representatives
(4) A member appointed under Subsection 3 shall have expertise in one or more of the following areas:
(a) artificial intelligence safety and alignment;
(b) machine learning engineering; or
(c) data privacy and cyber security.
(5) Membership shall be allocated such that each area of expertise is represented by at least 3 members
(6) (a) A member shall serve a 3 year term; and
(b) The terms of the member shall be staggered so that 3 board members are appointed every year
(7) The committee conducts annual audits of all approved artificial intelligence systems, which will include full access to:
(a) the entire codebase of the artificial intelligence system;
(b) all data used in the training of the system; and
(c) the model weights.
(8) To maintain the privacy of corporate trade secrets the committee shall:
(a) conduct all audits within an air-gapped, decentralized storage and audit environment;
(b) support receipt and verification of zero-knowledge audit proofs for contraband-data compliance without requiring public disclosure of proprietary source code, model weights, or trade secrets;
(c)  classify all information obtained under Subsection 7 as protected records under Title 63G, Chapter 2, Government Records Access and Management Act; and
(d) ensure that all third-party contracted engineers hired to assist the committee maintain an active SOC 2 Type II compliance certification.

**Section 4. Data Center Environmental and Reporting Standards**
(1) Any operator of a data center within the state shall comply with the standards established in this section
(2) Annual reporting requirements:
(a) No later than June 1st of each year, every operator shall submit to the Utah
Department of Environmental Quality a report covering the previous calendar
year that includes:
	(i) A total annual electricity consumption;
	(ii) A comprehensive analysis of utilized electricity sources;
(iii) Estimated greenhouse gas emissions for both direct and indirect emissions; and
(iv) A general description of the artificial intelligence systems trained or operated at the facility during the reporting period.
(b) This report shall be published by the Department of Environmental Quality upon review
(3) All new high-consumption data centers shall
	(i) utilize non-consumptive cooling techniques; 
	(ii) not withdraw water from the Great Salt Lake;
(iii) source at least 50% of annual electrical consumption from renewable or carbon-free sources by January 1, 2030; and
(iv) source 100% of annual electrical consumption from renewable or carbon-free	sources by January 1, 2035.

**Section 5. Data Center Siting, Zoning, and Security**
(1) No high-consumption data center shall commence construction within the state without first obtaining a siting permit issued by the Department of Environmental Quality
(2) Any application for a siting permit shall include:
(a) an environmental impact assessment addressing projected water consumption, electrical demand, and greenhouse gas emissions;
(b) a water availability assessment study prepared in coordination with the Division of Water Resources, ensuring the facility won’t impair the water supply of any municipality, agricultural, or ecological system within the affected area; and
(c) a grid impact study prepared in coordination with the public service commission, ensuring the facility will not compromise the reliability of electrical service to others in the area.
(3) Before issuing a siting permit, the Department of Environmental Quality shall:
	(a) Publicly publish evidence of the permit request;
	(b) Provide a public comment period of no fewer than 30 days; and
(c) Hold at least one public hearing in the county in which the proposed facility will be located.
(4) Each operator of a data center used to train or operate an artificial intelligence system shall:
(a) Maintain the infrastructure necessary to actuate a total deactivation of the data center capabilities;
(b) segregate systems designated as air-gapped from all other networked systems within the facility; and
(c) maintain a log of all transfers of data into or out of those systems.
(5) Any siting permit may be suspended or revoked upon finding of repeated or willful violation of these requirements.
